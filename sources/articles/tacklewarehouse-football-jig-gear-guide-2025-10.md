## Extracted findings
- **Core habitat framing:** football jig excels "anytime bass are keyed in on crawdads and relating to hard bottom" — explicitly calls out pea gravel, chunk rock, main lake points, bluff walls, channel swing banks, offshore humps, rock piles, offshore ledges, and "transitions" in bottom composition as the target zones. Directly matches Center Hill's rock/point structure.
- **Rod:** 7'–7'6", medium-heavy to heavy power, fast taper. Rationale: heavier power for lip penetration against heavy-gauge jig hooks; fast taper loads into backbone for a positive hookset; longer rod = more casting distance (covering water) and more hookset leverage at the end of a long cast.
- **Reel:** casting reel, 7:1–8:1 gear ratio, 100–200 size for line capacity with heavier line; external brake adjustment convenient.
- **Line:** fluorocarbon — sinks, keeps the jig in the strike zone, low stretch for bite detection/feel on a slow-moving bottom bait (contrasted with reaction baits where a fish gets only a split second to decide). 12–20 lb range, **15 lb as the general starting point**; heavier for abrasion resistance around rock, or lighter/smaller diameter to reach bottom faster in deep water.
- **Trailers/modifications:** trailer style is angler preference but the guide's rule of thumb — warm-water trailers should have MORE action (twin-tail grub, craw with big flapping appendages); cold-water trailers should be subtler (chunk, beaver-style). Named trailer SKUs: NetBait BaitFuel Paca Chunk, Zoom Ultra Vibe Speed Craw 3.5", Reaction Innovations Sweet Beaver 4.20, Strike King Rage Bug/Rage Tail Chunk, Berkley PowerBait Chigger Craw, 6th Sense Stroker Craw, Xzone Lures Pro Series Adrenaline Craws, Berkley PowerBait MaxScent Power Chunk. Trim/cut the weedguard fibers at the base to thin it and improve hookup ratio around sparse cover; trim the skirt to match trailer length or remove entirely in very clear water; carry rattle bands for stained water.
- **Tips:** warmer water → sharper rod-tip snaps for more action; colder water → slow down. When the jig bumps cover, don't rush to free it — shake it in place and bang it against the structure; this is a high-percentage moment. On the bite: reel down to the fish, use a long, powerful sweeping hookset, keep the hook driven while reeling in (heavy-gauge hooks need sustained pressure). Upsizing jig weight increases fall rate for reaction bites from aggressive fish; dyeing trailer appendages red or chartreuse when fish key on bluegill or red craws; scent additive helps in tough conditions.
