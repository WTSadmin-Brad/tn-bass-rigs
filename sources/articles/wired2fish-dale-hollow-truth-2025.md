## Extracted findings

**Seasonal pattern (Dale Hollow smallmouth):**
- Best windows: March–April (pre-spawn / spawn) and October–November (post-spawn / fall feeding).
- Spring: crankbaits (Spro RkCrawlers), bass jigs with plastic trailers — transition areas, gravel banks, rocky points.
- Post-spawn / late spring: topwater (Yank-Um Custom Tackle Peanut) over deep rocky points and main-lake weedbeds.
- Summer: forward-facing sonar to locate suspended fish chasing baitfish offshore.
- Fall: FFS targeting suspended schools in deep channels (5–20 ft down over deeper water); bass jigs with plastic trailers on bluff walls and rocky points.

**Structure that holds fish:** rocky main-lake and secondary points, bluff walls, deep channels, rocky banks and gravel spawning areas, weedbeds.

**Direct quotes:**
- **Guide Dave Clark:** "We have a lot of suspended smallmouths in Dale Hollow. We'll catch smallmouths in water as deep as 50 feet."
- **TWRA biologist Marcy Anderson:** "In our surveys, we'll still see fish 20 inches and over. And we still have good numbers, too."

**Night-fishing program (Guide Greg Brisendine, April–October):**
- 1/4-oz bucktail hair jig + Zoom Skinny Chunk trailer.
- 10-lb braid, 7-ft medium-heavy baitcaster.
- "Around the first of June, you have to throw right up to the bank."

**Slot limit:** 16–21 inches protected (since 2000); one over 21" and one under 16" daily.

**Lake stats:** ~22,700–27,700 acres; 620 miles of shoreline; deep, clear water reservoir.
