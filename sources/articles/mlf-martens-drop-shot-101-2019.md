## Extracted findings

### Mainline
- 16-lb **Sunline SX1** braided line on a spinning rod.

### Leader
- 8- to 10-lb fluorocarbon (brand not stated; Martens was a Sunline pro at the time — likely Sunline FC Sniper / Shooter).
- Length framework (Martens):
  - "On average, I would say 8 to 10 inches is probably your best bet."
  - Dirty water — 1 to 2 inches (shortest he uses).
  - Clear water with 20–30 ft visibility — 2- to 3-foot leaders.

### Hook
- **Gamakatsu G-Finesse Heavy Cover Hook.** "The bait can vary, but the hook stays consistent: Martens goes with a Gamakatsu G-Finesse Heavy Cover Hook when drop-shotting." (Article does not specify size; the G-Finesse Heavy Cover line is a Texas-style hook for cover/Texas-rigged drop-shot use, not a nose-hook open drop-shot hook.)

### Weight
- 3/16-oz "normally."
- Drop to 1/16-oz or 1/8-oz when no wind and water is clear.
- Up to 1/4-oz when fish are aggressive or conditions are tougher.

### Knots
- **Double Uni** at the hook. Explicitly rejects Palomar at the hook for drop-shot:
  - "A lot of anglers still use a Palomar knot to tie their drop-shots and that's just not ideal. A Palomar knot can take so much punishment from the fish when you set the hook because there's no weight in front of it, it's just straight line. What I choose to tie instead is a Double Uni knot, it works better for me and is more reliable."

### Where it works (Martens's framing)
- "I tend to throw drop-shots less often when I'm fishing in the southern United States. If there's a really muddy lake with dirty water, then it's usually not the best place to use a drop-shot. Since a drop-shot suspends instead of hitting the bottom, it's harder for a fish to locate in dingy water. You need clear water with good visibility to be able to use one effectively. That's why it's more of a northern-style bait."
