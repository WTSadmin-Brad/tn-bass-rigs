## Extracted findings

### Brandon Palaniuk — drop-shot for cruising bass
- **Bait:** Roboworm finesse worm.
- **Hook:** #2 drop-shot hook (brand not stated in this 2016 piece — by the 2022 A-List Palaniuk had standardized on VMC; in 2015 Bassmaster he was on VMC #1; reasonable read is VMC family across his career).
- **Weight:** **1/8 or 3/16-oz tungsten round weight,** **6 inches** below the hook.
  - Reason for round shape: "He prefers the round weight because he believes it sticks in a mud bottom better than other weight shapes."
- **Mainline:** 8-lb FireLine.
- **Leader:** 6- or 8-lb Berkley 100% Fluorocarbon.
- **Rod:** 6 1/2- to 7-foot medium light spinning rod.
- **Reel:** Size 20 spinning reel.

### Palaniuk — technique for cruising bass
- "When I see a bass roaming in the shallows" — leads the fish with a long cast.
- "Palaniuk will follow the fish until he gets into a favorable casting position. 'I have had times where I have chased one fish for hundreds of yards just following it down the bank.'"
- Trolling motor: low constant, not switching on/off (which spooks fish).
- "Palaniuk ensures his weight rests on the bottom before the fish moves within 3 feet of his lure. Then he starts shaking the rig for as long as a minute."
- Stationary lure: "wants his lure stationary because he notices bass tend to spook when the bait moves forward."
- Best seasons for the cruising-bass drop-shot pattern: spring (around bluegill beds) and fall (busting shad in shallows).
- Context: 2012 Elite Series Green Bay 2nd-place finish using this on smallmouth coming off a flat next to a subtle drop in the afternoon.

### Dean Rojas — drop-shot for cruising bass
- **Bait:** hand-poured finesse worms (prefers hand-poured for softer feel and longer fish hold time).
- **Hook:** **2/0 Extra Wide Gap (EWG) Gamakatsu hook.**
- **Weight:** 3/16-oz mojo-style weight (cylinder-shaped weight specifically used on a Carolina-style mojo rig — distinct from drop-shot pencil/teardrop/cylinder finesse weights).
- **Leader:** 8 to 10 inches below hook.
- **Rod:** 6'2" or 6'6" spinning rod.
- **Line:** 6- or 8-lb fluorocarbon (single line, not braid+leader system).
- Boat distance: keeps boat at least 15 ft from the fish; long cast leading the fish.
- Technique: shake on slack line; if fish ignores, lift-and-drop, sometimes drag.

## Notes

- Tier B + `[STALE]` (September 2016). Two named pros, sponsorship-disparate (Palaniuk on Berkley/VMC era; Rojas on Gamakatsu/Strike King). The 2016 Palaniuk specifics align with his 2015 Bassmaster setup but are noticeably different from his 2022 A-List setup — line gear has evolved heavier braid mainline (15-lb Smackdown) and fluoro leader (Seaguar Gold Label/Tatsu) over the decade. The cruising-bass-specific 6" leader is shorter than his standard 10–12" in `bassmaster-drop-shot-tips-from-pros-2015.md` — that's the cruising application driving the leader length down, consistent with the Iaconelli "shallow / spawning fish / low cover → short leader" framework (`mikeiaconelli-blog-mastering-dropshot-2024.md`).
- The cruising-bass pattern is dossier-relevant for the "When it excels" and "Cadence/retrieve" sections — specifically the spring-bluegill-bed and fall-shallow-shad applications. **Direct TN application:** Dale Hollow / Center Hill / Norris smallmouth cruising shallow flats in spring and fall is exactly this pattern. `[TN-LOCAL]` extrapolation, but the empirical mechanism (leading fish, shaking stationary lure) is condition-independent.
- The Rojas 2/0 EWG Gamakatsu + hand-poured worm + 3/16-oz mojo is a different style from the modern Bubba Shot — same upsized-cover concept but with a different hook profile. Worth a sub-bullet showing this older iteration of the cover/cruising hybrid before the Owner Cover Shot dominance.
- "Round weight sticks in mud bottom better" is a fourth weight-shape rationale (cylinder = no twist / cover; teardrop = best feel; round = mud-anchor; pencil = rock). Worth surfacing all four with their condition-specific reasoning.
- Independent corroboration of the "shake stationary, don't move forward" technique — Aaron Martens deadstick (`bassmaster-5-drop-shot-tactics-2017.md`), Ehrler drop-shot drag is the contrast (`bassmaster-5-drop-shot-tactics-2017.md`). Stationary-shake is the dominant cadence; drag is the situational variant.
