## Extracted findings (hydrological/operational context only — trout content excluded per scope)

> "The spring drought means we were able to retain a lot of cold water rather than generate it for flood control. Compared to last year, we have significantly higher dissolved oxygen and water temperatures are cooler. The reservoir is also in great shape which means, barring a hurricane, we should have decent water quality throughout the summer. It's still too early to make any assessments about the fall."

> "Throughout the week we have been seeing orifice only (great for wading) in the morning and the generators turning on in the early afternoon through the early evening. On the weekend you can expect mostly orifice but a handful of hours of generation in the afternoon and low water in the morning. Be sure to check before you leave because sometimes on particularly hot days, they will need additional generation to meet power demand."

Dam-release water temp note (tailwater discharge, not reservoir surface): "Water temperatures are still 48F coming out of the dam" (as of 2026-07-16) — this is hypolimnetic release temp, relevant only to understand that generation pulls cold, deep reservoir water through the dam; not a reservoir surface-temperature reading.
