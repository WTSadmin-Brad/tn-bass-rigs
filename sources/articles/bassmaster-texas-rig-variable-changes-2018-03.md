## Extracted findings
- Tungsten vs. lead: tungsten is roughly twice the density of lead, so a given weight is more compact/smaller-diameter than the lead equivalent — cited advantage for slipping through cover and for flipping matted vegetation/heavy cover. Tungsten's greater hardness also transmits bottom composition (rock/wood/hard vs. soft bottom) more clearly through the line than lead does. Attributed claim: B.A.S.S. Opens champion Trevor Fitzgerald (whose company produces tungsten weights) — [PRO-CLAIM, SPONSOR-FLAG].
- Beads: a glass/metal bead between weight and hook eye adds a "clacking" noise on the cast/fall meant to mimic feeding baitfish (bream); commonly removed in high-pressure situations for a quieter presentation.
- Pegging: pegging the weight (bobber stopper or toothpick) keeps weight and bait together, which the author says most benefits fishing in cover/grass by stopping the weight from sliding up the line and letting the bait penetrate the cover. Author's own preference is to fish an **unpegged** weight on large worms or creature baits in **open water** specifically because the free-sliding weight reaches bottom first and lets the plastic spiral down for extra fall action — direct contrast to a pegged rig's flatter, faster, together-fall.
