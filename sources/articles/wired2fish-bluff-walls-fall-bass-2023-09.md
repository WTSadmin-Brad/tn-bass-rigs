## Extracted findings
- Bluffs are productive in fall because they give bass instant access to both shallow and deep water on one piece of structure — described as a "highway" fish use to move up/down the water column and chase bait toward creek backs.
- Current matters: bluffs on channel-swing banks that receive current concentrate fish tight to the wall, waiting for bait washed down the rocks — flipping a jig or Texas rig right against the wall is named as effective in that scenario. With little/no current, fish roam off the bluff and are better targeted with sonar + shad-imitating baits (jerkbait, swimbait, Alabama rig).
- "Sweet spot" concept: fish spread across a whole bluff but concentrate at one high-percentage feature — a point, a tree, or a depression on the wall — worth hitting multiple times per day; author's home-river example found the sweet spot to be the stretch of bluff that faced upriver into the current.
- Bait selection for bluffs (author's short list): Texas rig (his most-used bluff bait — cites a Reaction Innovations Sweet Beaver on a 5/16 oz weight for a slower fall, works wood and rock), jerkbait (thrown **parallel to the bluff wall**, bounced off the rock between jerks, low-current scenario), topwater walking bait/spook (**parallel the bluff, plus long casts off the points of the bluff**), and an Alabama rig (thrown parallel to the wall to call fish out, or at individually-marked roaming fish in low current).
- Casting-angle takeaway used in this report's boat-position synthesis: **parallel-to-the-wall casts** keep a bait in the strike zone longest along a vertical bluff face; **casts off the point/end of the bluff** cover the transition where the wall breaks to a point or flat.
