## Extracted findings — resolves the "orifice gate open" question for item 3

This is the single most important find for interpreting the 2026 TVA notice "Center Hill orifice gate will be open until further notice" (effective 06/01/2026). It is **NOT evidence of a dam-safety emergency or the low-pool situation** — it documents a routine, **annual operational cycle**:

> "On Monday, June 10th [2024], the Corps reinstalled the orifice gate at Center Hill Dam, which was previously removed in the fall for inspection. The reinstallation of the gate establishes a new summer/fall minimum flow regime... aimed at enhancing the habitat of the renowned cold-water trout fishery situated below the dam on Caney Fork River."

Quote (Robert Dillingham, hydraulic engineer, Nashville District Water Management Section):

> "In response to substantial public input, the Corps has set a new seasonal minimum continuous flow of 250 cubic feet per second from the dam. This strategic adjustment seeks to augment the wetted perimeter of the stream, mitigating the risk of elevated temperatures during periods of zero generation and thereby fostering a conducive environment for trout and other aquatic life."

Mechanics: the orifice gate sits ~120 ft deep in the forebay; it is **removed each fall for inspection**, then **reinstalled the following June** (2024 case: reinstalled June 10, effective through November) once high-water risk has passed. Crane-and-underwater-rover operation, historically using a crane loaned from Wolf Creek Dam.

**Implication for 2026:** The pattern (remove fall → reinstall following June, effective through Nov) matches exactly the TVA notice's "orifice gate open... effective 06/01/2026." This is very likely the **same annual tailwater-trout minimum-flow program continuing in 2026**, not a new or emergency dam-safety measure. It explains continuous orifice release volumes (contributing to the ~1,030 cfs average daily outflow noted in shared context) as baseline minimum-flow compliance for the Caney Fork trout fishery below the dam — **a tailwater management purpose, separate from reservoir-side bass fishing**, though its outflow does contribute to the reservoir's fall drawdown rate.

**Caveat:** this specific article is dated 2024, not 2026 — no 2026-dated USACE statement was found that explicitly re-confirms the 250 cfs minimum-flow figure or reinstallation date for 2026 specifically (searches for a 2026 version returned nothing new; see Fetch-failure log in main report). Treat "same program, continuing in 2026" as a well-supported inference from an annual, named, dated program, not as directly re-confirmed for the current year.

## Notes

Confirms the orifice gate outflow purpose is tailwater trout habitat (cold-water fishery on Caney Fork below the dam), unrelated to bass angling on the Center Hill reservoir itself, though it is one of the two outflow components (orifice + generation) that shape reservoir drawdown rate cited in shared-context.
