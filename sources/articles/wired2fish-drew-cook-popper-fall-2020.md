## Extracted findings
- Drew Cook's core claim: "In the fall, the fish are keying on shad... A popper is a really great way to fish targets... you can throw a popper all the way up to rock, or a tree, or whatever you're targeting and pop it, stop, and the bait stays in the strike zone a lot longer than a walking-style bait" — a walking bait puts you "out of the strike zone" ~3 seconds after it lands; a popper lets you work a target repeatedly.
- Bait: **SPRO E-Pop 80** (1/2 oz, 80 mm) — concave popper face for surface disturbance, elongated body also walks like a stick bait; long-casting, useful "when you're around schooling fish." Stock hooks: No. 4 Gamakatsu treble (front), No. 4 Gamakatsu feather treble (rear) — Cook stresses a dressed/feathered rear hook matters even when the bait is sitting still between pops.
- Colors: "In the fall, plain white works the best, with some colored accents like blue and red, or chartreuse for smallmouth."
- Presentation: pop-pause, let rings settle, pop again; slow to 3–4 sec between pops on a tough bite; on a following/boiling fish, give 3–4 quick twitches to trigger the reaction strike. Works well as a follow-up bait behind a walking bait or buzzbait after a missed blowup.
- Tackle: **Dobyns 705 Champion XP** rod, **14–20 lb Seaguar monofilament**, 7.1:1 gear ratio reel — chosen for target-casting comfort/accuracy over distance, since most popper fishing is to visible targets, not blind casting.
- Secondary bait for grass: SPRO Bronzeye Poppin' Frog 60 on a heavier 734 Champion XP rod / 50 lb braid / 8.5:1 reel (not relevant to Center Hill — no grass).

## Notes
- [STALE] 2020 sourcing but named-pro, high-density, internally consistent, and the "popper stays in strike zone longer than a walker" mechanism is a durable, non-perishable claim — corroborates DeFoe (2026) and Walker Smith (2020) that a popper is not merely a fallback but often the SHARPER choice on visible schoolers, contra the generic "walking bait for schoolers" assumption.
- Mono (not fluoro/braid) main line for the popper — floats, matches topwater need to keep the bait high; do not confuse with the jighead-minnow braid/leader spec from a different technique.
