## Extracted findings

Dated USACE statement (2026-04-21) explaining why Cumberland Basin lakes, including Center Hill, are running low heading into summer 2026: **lack of substantial spring rainfall / ongoing drought**, not (primarily) dam construction.

> "Due to lack of substantial spring rainfall across the Cumberland River Basin, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Nashville District continues to manage seasonal low lake elevations at multiple dam projects... The water management team is currently operating at minimum releases, holding back water to keep lake levels as high as possible... The lack of rain has resulted in spring elevations rarely seen in recent decades."

Reservoir table as of ~2026-04-20 (weekly drop rate, historical April 20 median, context):

Quote (David Bogema, chief of water management, Nashville District): "We are holding on to as much of that water as we can while still meeting our hydropower and navigation requirements... The Nashville District is making the best use of the limited water that we have right now."

Operational note: in May 2026, weekly hydropower demand was expected to drop ~30%, allowing the district to cut water releases ~30% to slow the rate of decline (consistent with CWMS data showing Center Hill still climbing from 636.1 ft on 4/20 up to a ~641.2 ft June peak, before resuming decline through summer/fall — see `usace-cwms-center-hill-elevation-september-history.md`).

**Key interpretive point for the tournament brief:** This dated primary source attributes 2026's low Center Hill pool to **drought / lack of spring rain**, not to the TVA orifice-gate note or DVIDS gate-replacement-project separately found (see `dvidshub-center-hill-spillway-gate-replacement-2026.md`). Both a drought-driven conservative-release posture AND an active gate-replacement/dam-infrastructure project appear to be in play for 2026; this article only speaks to the drought/reservoir-management side. It also confirms the "lowered for construction" reference at Center Hill is historically anchored to **2019**, matching the TWRA page's mention of a "multi-year drawdown... to facilitate work on the dam" expected to resume full pool "in 2021" (see `twra-center-hill-reservoir-page-2026.md`) — i.e., that TWRA drawdown episode is the 2019-vintage construction event, a separate and earlier episode from whatever is driving 2026's even-lower reading.
