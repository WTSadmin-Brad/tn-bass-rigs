## Extracted findings
- **Mike Iaconelli (rigging):** Weight = snag-resistant cylindrical shape (bell-sinker/round-eye slides more freely but he prefers cylindrical). Sizes: 1/8–1/4 oz in water ≤10 ft; 5/16–3/8 oz from 10–25 ft; up to 1/2 oz in deeper water or heavy current. Bobber stop placed ahead of a **Berkley Fusion19 hook, 2/0–4/0** sized to bait — light/medium wire only ("forget heavy hooks... you need a light- or medium-wire hook to allow the bait to fall naturally"). Bobber stop also lets him fish it "more like a Carolina rig" by sliding the stop up to set leader length. Baits: **Berkley PowerBait MaxScent The General** (soft stickbait, 5" junebug scored a Top 20 on the Potomac), **Berkley PowerBait MaxScent Creature Hawg**, beaver/craw styles; also flags the newer **Berkley PowerBait MaxScent Flux-Gill** (flat teardrop panfish profile, holes for scent dispersal, "super-slow gliding fall") as his pick when bass feed on panfish. [SPONSOR-FLAG]
- **Two bite windows (Iaconelli):** ~50% of bites on the initial fall/flutter after the weight hits bottom (give it several seconds); ~50% happen the instant the bait bumps into the sinker — he tested this in a pool and saw the bait "pop up like a crawfish in a defense stance." Because bites cluster at fall/contact, he does NOT drag after contact — he rips the rig back up and lets it fall slack again, repeating through the strike zone rather than dragging.
- **John Crews (rigging/technique):** Uses **Spro Tungsten Pea Sinker** (line runs through an internal tube — snag-resistant, protects line), 1/4–3/8 oz for most presentations. Baits: **Missile Baits D Bomb** and **Baby D Stroyer** ("bait needs good resistance to create separation after the cast"). In water ≤8 ft he free-falls on slack line; in deeper water he strips extra line off the reel to guarantee separation as the weight sinks. Confirms bait-hits-weight is a trigger; if no bite on fall/contact he drags a few feet like a shaky/swing head, then rips it back up to repeat. Best on rock, stumps, wood cover — home water Smith Mountain Lake VA; also produced at 2025 Elite events on Lake Hartwell and Lake Fork. States it works in clear water best but "also works in stained water, just as a drop shot does."
- **Bryan Schmitt (current/yo-yo variant, not primary for our no-current scenario):** Uses 1/2 oz+ weight to anchor bottom in current, yo-yos the bait to/from the weight; **Reins Heavy Weight TG Down Shot Sinker** with a round metal eye that swivels (needed in current so the line doesn't twist around the weight). Bait: **Missile Baits Baby D Stroyer**. Not directly applicable — Center Hill has no current during tournament hours per conditions brief — but confirms Missile Baits Baby D Stroyer as a cross-pro-endorsed free-rig creature bait (2 of 3 pros in this article name it).
- **General:** Free rig = unpegged, drop-shot-style weight that free-slides on the line ahead of the bait, distinct from an unpegged Texas rig bullet weight (which is nose-heavy/hydrodynamic and falls at an angle rather than straight down) — same core mechanism TacticalBassin's Tim Little described.

## Notes
- This is the strongest free-rig source in the packet: two named, on-record Elite Series pros (Iaconelli, Crews) giving exact hook models/sizes, weight ranges by depth band, and a shared/cross-validated bait (Missile Baits Baby D Stroyer/D Bomb) — converges with TacticalBassin's independent pick of the same Missile Baits family for creature-style free rigging.
- Iaconelli's 10–25 ft weight band (5/16–3/8 oz) and Crews's rock/stump preference line up closely with the free rig's fit for Center Hill's 20–30 ft rock points and Brad's 25-ft rocky spot.
