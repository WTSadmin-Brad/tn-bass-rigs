## Extracted findings

**Reservoir facts:** Created 1948 (Center Hill Dam on Caney Fork River), USACE-operated. 18,220 surface acres. Spans DeKalb, Putnam, Warren, White counties. Rocky shorelines, steep ridges. Gamefish: black bass, crappie, walleye, bluegill, catfish.

**Current black bass regulations (as posted):**
- Black bass (combined): 5/day
- Largemouth: 15-inch minimum length limit
- Smallmouth: 18-inch minimum length limit
- Spotted bass: no length limit
- Special restriction: Jan 1–Apr 30, Caney Fork River from Rock Island State Park ramp upstream to Great Falls Dam — single-hook/single-point lure only, max 3 rods/poles per angler (this stretch is upstream tailwater/tributary, not the tournament reservoir area).

**Species present / forage (TWRA narrative, undated/stale but no contradicting newer source found):**
- Largemouth, smallmouth, and spotted bass all present and TWRA-surveyed via biannual spring electrofishing + annual roving creel.
- Smallmouth: "above average" density per spring electrofishing, good multi-year spawning success; compared favorably to Dale Hollow-type rocky/bluff/point habitat.
- Spotted bass: catch rates "currently below average," possible reproduction decline over the prior 5 years (as of original authoring).
- **No mention of Alabama bass anywhere on the page** — TWRA lists only largemouth/smallmouth/spotted for Center Hill; Alabama bass status remains unconfirmed by this source (shared-context flag stands).
- Forage: page discusses shad ("schools of shad") as the dominant late-fall/winter forage trigger across largemouth, smallmouth, and spotted bass sections; bluegill also present as prey/panfish. No explicit shad species (threadfin vs. gizzard) named, and no mention of alewife.
- Crappie: black crappie (incl. "black nose" crappie strain, TWRA stocking program since 1990) dominant; white crappie a small % of population, more upper-reservoir.
- Walleye: TWRA stocks fingerlings annually; spawning run mid-March, Blue Hole area of Caney Fork near Rock Island State Park (upstream, not tournament area).

**Historical drawdown / dam-work note (directly relevant to elevation-history findings — [CONTESTED]/[STALE]):**
> "Spawning success for largemouth bass has been low overall as compared to years past. However, this could be due in large part to an extended, multi-year drawdown of Center Hill Reservoir to facilitate work on the dam, which has left historic spawning grounds high and dry. Center Hill Reservoir is expected to resume full summer pool capacity in 2021..."

This confirms Center Hill underwent a **multi-year engineered drawdown for dam remediation work**, historically, with an expectation (as of original authoring, ~2019–2020) that full summer pool (648 ft) would resume by 2021. The USACE CWMS pull (see `usace-cwms-center-hill-elevation-september-history.md`) shows the reservoir has NOT been reaching 648 ft even in June in the years sampled (peak 641.2 ft in June 2026) — this is a direct contradiction/open question: either (a) the TWRA page is stale and never updated after "resume in 2021" didn't fully materialize, (b) the dam remediation work restarted/continued past 2021, or (c) 648 ft is not actually being targeted as "normal" full pool anymore. No newer TWRA statement found to resolve this.

**Species spawning temperature thresholds (useful for thermal-stage inference, not measurements):**
- Largemouth: 68–72°F
- Smallmouth: 59–60°F
- Spotted bass: 63–68°F
- Crappie: black 62–68°F, white 60–65°F
- Bluegill: 70–75°F
- Catfish: 75–80°F (spawn)
- Walleye: 45–50°F
- Muskellunge: 49–50°F

**Seasonal pattern notes (generic TWRA guidance, not dated/measured):**
- Largemouth: "Once the water warms up to the '80s, most anglers change to night fishing which will last through August." Post-spawn: standing structure, topwater, soft plastics. Summer (80s water): spinnerbaits/jigs/soft plastics in 10–20 ft, night. Late fall (cooling water): bass follow shad schools; jigging spoons under shad around Thanksgiving.
- Smallmouth: Once water hits 80s, night fishing with spinnerbaits/jigs/soft plastics in 5–10 ft; early topwater at sunrise near cover; deep bite 15–25 ft jigs/soft plastics during "dog days." Fall cooldown: smallmouth move to shallower long tapering points near dam, crankbaits and Zara Spooks; late fall, jigging spoons/Zara Spooks near shad schools in creeks near the dam.
- Spotted bass: summer = nighttime bite, spinnerbaits/topwater buzzbaits/jerkbaits. Cooling water (60s): focus schools of shad, jig spoons under shad in creeks on the lower end of the lake around Thanksgiving.

These are generic/undated seasonal guidance from the TWRA narrative, not dated field observations — useful only as background pattern context, not as evidence rows.
