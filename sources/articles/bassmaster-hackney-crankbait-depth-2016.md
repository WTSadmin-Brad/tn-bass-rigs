## Extracted findings
- **Line size is the #1 depth factor.** Thinner line = deeper running. Standard recommendation: 10 or 12 lb fluorocarbon "will handle most situations and give you a reasonable depth for most lures."
- **Bigger/heavier baits need heavier line for structural reasons** even though it costs depth: an 8XD or 10XD (6", ~2 oz) needs 14 lb test minimum — lighter line won't hold up to the resistance/weight of casting and cranking a bait that large.
- **Reel speed affects depth**: faster retrieve = deeper running. Gear ratio number is deceptive — a 7.3:1 reel only retrieves at that ratio with a full spool; after a long cast or as line pays out, effective ratio (and thus retrieve speed) drops.
- **Spool size affects retrieve speed**: bigger spool (e.g., 300 size vs 200/100) picks up more line per turn → faster effective retrieve → more depth, independent of gear ratio number.
- **The bait must "run true"** to reach max depth — at high speed a bait can visibly-imperceptibly run slightly to one side, costing depth even when it looks straight at medium speed.
- **Cast length is a major factor**: longer casts = deeper running. Pros routinely out-cast recreational anglers, which is part of why pro-reported running depths look optimistic.
- **Practical fixes**: pick a bait rated deeper than the actual target depth (e.g., a 12-14 ft bait to reach a 10 ft bottom) to compensate for imperfect technique; add slightly bigger/shorter-shank replacement hooks for a little extra weight/depth; long-lining is not legal in Bass Elite Series competition (per his note, "so far as I know") — relevant precedent that added static line-out-before-cast tricks are a grey/banned area in some formats, not needed here since Mayson/Noah fish youth-adjacent local TN circuit rules, not BASS Elite.

## Notes
- This is the general mechanical framework (line size, reel speed/spool, true-tracking, cast length) used to reason about 5XD/6XD/DD22 running depth on 10-12 lb fluoro for the Center Hill brief — no lake-specific or bait-specific (5XD/6XD/DD22) numbers are given here; those come from the Wired2Fish/Combs source cached separately.
