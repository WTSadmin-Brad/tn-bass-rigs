## Extracted findings
- **Urchin baits (soft plastic finesse worm/creature, "Coike"-style)**: still producing in September. Fished around docks, vegetation, rocks, cover. Smaller shad-color baits on drop shot when bass relate to shad; switch to 20mm bait in green pumpkin or other dark color when bass are "on bream." Weedless Cradlehead (Picasso) noted for cover.
- **Topwater**: buzzbaits, poppers, Berkley Ploppers, Spooks, frogs, toads all work in September, both on schooling shad busts in open water and on bream-feeding bass along the bank. Open-water schoolers: use long-casting Spook/Sammy/pencil popper (poppers don't reach). Bank/vegetation bass: frog, toad, or buzzbait over treble-hook baits. Match hatch to baitfish size. Watch for surface activity — actual blowups or "nervous water"/minnows skipping.
- **Spinnerbait** — called "the GOAT of fall fishing," versatility-driven: double willow blade for shad-relating bass near the surface; tandem Colorado/willow for bass in stalky bank vegetation; double Colorado for shallow muddy water. Can also be fished at night through September. Best on overcast days with wind chop; pump the retrieve (irregular blade flutter/burst triggers strikes vs. straight retrieve) — directly matches Center Hill's forecast overcast/showery, calm-to-light-wind Saturday.
- **Glide baits (Berkley Chop Block)**: described as a September pattern "in the south" for big, infrequent bites — fished shallow around docks/laydowns/cover, valued for its soft-plastic skip-ability under docks/bushes/pontoons to reach pressured big fish. [SPONSOR-FLAG].
- **Chris Johnston section (Great Lakes-focused, north US)** — DISCOUNTED for TN highland-reservoir transfer; rigging detail on "Coike" (soft jerk/glide minnow) with Fullcast + frog hook + teardrop 1/8oz split-ring weight for largemouth, single Gamakatsu Bead Hook + nail weight for smallmouth; topwater (SPRO E Pop) over suspended bream/bluegill/perch relating to deeper grass — habitat (grass) doesn't match Center Hill (no grass), so pattern itself is lower-transferability even though bait/rig detail is exact.
- **Brandon Cobb (South Carolina)** — HIGH transferability, southeast highland/piedmont reservoir angler: describes "two populations" of September bass. (1) Shallow/creek-and-river fish moving toward fall pattern — fish wood, docks, cover, pitching a Zoom Brush Hog (creature bait) or a buzzbait (his own Greenfish Toad Toter model, Gray Ghost color, with a Horny Toad trailer) — described as "an intermediate time where some are eating bluegills and some are eating shad," few bites but big ones. (2) Suspended, bait-following fish in the middle-to-lower lake, over any depth, following shad balls — targeted by live-scoping with a minnow or jerkbait (species/technique not further specified: no brand/size given for the jerkbait). Locate by watching for bait balling/idling on top, especially mornings and afternoons, before fish show themselves.
