## Extracted findings
- **Core technique**: burning a bladed jig ("Thump Nugget," 3/8 oz, described as small-bodied so it stays balanced at high speed) — retrieved as fast as possible just under/bulging the surface, not breaking it, in clear water over/near vegetation and "drains" (small creek/ditch inflow points into the ponds).
- **Rationale for burning**: (1) reaction-strike trigger from speed; (2) some bass that won't hit a true topwater (broken-surface bait) will still hit a bladed jig burned just below the surface — host's theory is some bass "don't like" the surface being broken but will react to something moving fast just under it; (3) general summer principle stated explicitly: "fish the extremes... either super duper fast or super duper slow" rather than a moderate/typical retrieve speed, to get more bites.
- **Water clarity caveat (self-stated)**: burning works better in clearer water; in muddy water this fast/subsurface presentation is described as a "sight thing" that's less effective, so he'd lean on other techniques in stained water.
- **Trailer/rigging**: "M Bait Siege Craw" trailer threaded PERPENDICULAR to the hook shank (jig-style threading), not parallel — stated as a specific, deliberate rigging detail for the bladed jig.
- **Gear**: switched from a fast-action all-graphite jig rod to a composite (half-glass/half-graphite) parabolic-bend rod ("Arc Tharp Series B Hit," 7'4", medium-heavy) specifically to reduce lost fish on a bladed jig — reasoning given: fast graphite lets you feel/react and set the hook too quickly/hard, resulting in shallow hookups (often just the tip) and more fish thrown on the retrieve/at the boat/bank; the softer composite rod loads more before the hookset registers, driving the hook deeper.
- **Habitat/location pattern**: bass concentrated at "drains" (small inflow points/ditches) in both ponds — "every single one we've been to, there's been fish around them." In the pond with heavy shoreline vegetation/scum, bites came within ~10 ft of the scum line; in the second, sparser-vegetation pond, bites instead came from the middle/deepest part where fish sought "overhead cover" substitute or just deeper comfort — implies bass position relative to available overhead/shade cover, defaulting to the deepest available water when shoreline cover is absent.
- **Size result**: one bass "probably 4 and 1/2 lbs" (described as "over four... probably pushing five pounds") caught on the Thump Nugget/Siege Craw combo out of the first pond; most others were sub-1 lb ("dink[s]") in the first pond, 1-2.5 lb range in the second (lower-density, larger-average-size) pond. No largemouth-vs-other-species distinction beyond one joking aside that a fish "looks like a true northern New York Michigan type largemouth" (small mouth on the fish, still identified as largemouth, not smallmouth — ambiguous phrasing).
- **Backup bait**: a "teeny tiny... bug bait" (topwater popper/bug, exact brand/model not stated) used to follow up on fish that wouldn't commit to the bladed jig, worked in the same drain areas.
- No forage cue named (no shad/bream mention). No explicit species confirmation beyond largemouth assumed. Time of day only the ~11:00 start; no evening/dusk data. No mention of wind. Rain arrived mid-session ("I'm getting rained on... now I'm getting soaked") — bite did not obviously change on the video's own account, though the narration doesn't isolate a pre/post-rain comparison.
