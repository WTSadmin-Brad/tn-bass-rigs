## Extracted findings

- **Core thesis:** A slow dragging retrieve — maintaining bottom contact and a fairly tight line — is the most effective drop-shot retrieve. The weight moving along the bottom imparts the action on the lure as it works through and over bottom composition.
- **Key counterintuitive point:** "It is so easy to impart too much action on a drop shot." Even when the angler tries to hold still, natural water movement, wave action, and arm motion provide plenty of lure action. The effective retrieve does *less*, not more.
- **Lure 1 — Baby Z2 (minnow/baitfish imitator):** A simple drag with the rod sweeping ever so slowly produces "incredible lifelike movement." When the weight catches on something then pops free, the lure pops too — a built-in trigger.
- **Lure 2 — Sexy Bug / Raid two-way (creature/JDM-style):** Dragged the same way, the tentacles produce alive-looking movement. The drop shot specifically excels at getting fish that get up close to commit to the strike.
- **Lure 3 — Drop-shot worm:** Same slow drag. Especially good through vegetation — the weight bouncing through and snagging vegetation produces the same pop-and-release action that rocks do.
- **Leader-length tie-in:** The dragging retrieve at a long-cast angle is precisely where a longer leader earns its keep — keeps the lure up off the bottom a bit more when brought back at an angle. "The leader for this video is about 3 feet in length."
