## Extracted findings

- **Setup he describes on-camera:** 3-inch YUM Dinger as the soft plastic, with "a bigger egg sinker that you can just get from Walmart" — chosen because the heavier weight will hold up against the edges of current and not get pulled away. (Note: this is an unconventional drop-shot weight choice — egg sinker rather than the standard pencil/cylinder tungsten. Treat as a curiosity, not a recommended spec.)
- **Retrieve described:** "I'm just twitching the rod tip just a little bit to kind of make that worm dance, that's all I'm doing — and then if you want, you just let off and give it just a little bit of slack." Author describes the slack-line moment as "a good way to get bites, especially finesse bites." This corroborates the "less action is more" principle from The Bass Fishing Life retrieve video (2025-08).
- **Conditions framing:** Cold-front-passing-through, 40°F air over 72°F constant-temp water (likely a spring-fed park pond). Hypothesis was that warm water would mean active fish despite the front; reality was that fish "spread out" and the drop shot underperformed.
- **Limitations:** Anecdotal, single creator, single short session, no controlled comparison. The "biggest school" footage that the title promises is not really showcased in the transcript — most of the video is setup narration, an interruption, and one fish capture.
