## Extracted findings
- **Core seasonal claim**: August/September/October is "fall transition" window across the country. Historically (pre-forward-facing sonar) anglers won this time of year by running up rivers/creek backs to stained, cooler, incoming water and fishing small crankbaits/buzzbaits/flipping. Now (with FFS) the better pattern on TN highland lakes (explicitly: Tennessee River chain, Douglas, Cherokee, Norris) is that many bass — often the biggest ones — sit in the MAIN LAKE, suspended, relating to nothing but food (shad), not to any structure/cover/depth contour.
- **Depth behavior**: fish observed at highly variable depths with no relationship to bottom depth — DeFoe: caught 2-3 lb fish at Norris "5 ft deep... 12 ft deep... 25 ft deep" while his boat sat over 30-90 ft of water; "the depth was irrelevant" to bottom, fish depth is what matters, and it is genuinely relative to the water column, not the lake bottom.
- **Movement**: suspended fall-transition fish are typically ACTIVELY SWIMMING/moving (not holding tight or slow-creeping) — described as swimming "one direction or another" in singles, doubles, or schools cruising in open water.
- **Baits/rigging, exact as stated**:
  - Primary: soft plastic swimbait/minnow-style bait ("Bass Pro Rock and Shad" — his brand) on a jighead, sizes 3", 4", 5" (avoiding "real big stuff" this time of year unless targeting trophy fish or a specific fishery calls for it).
  - Jighead weights: 1/8 oz, 3/16 oz, 3/8 oz explicitly named as a range he keeps rigged, matched to how deep/how far the suspended fish are — heavier head for deeper/farther fish, lighter head (e.g., 3/8 oz paired with a small minnow) to keep pace with fish higher/closer in the water column so it reaches them before they move off.
  - Preferred jighead models: VMC "Redline" tungsten head (favorite, describes it as hard to find/worth stockpiling) and VMC "Minnow Shaker" head, hook size "2/0" as most-used, trims back the weed-guard/keeper because "it's got so much there that you don't need it all."
  - Topwater alternative when fish are shallow in the water column or visibly busting: a "chug bug" style popper — specifically calls out a "chrome and blue Storm Chug Bug" as unusually effective "that time of year"; also names walking baits generally as an option.
  - Explicitly says soft "urchin"/"spiky ball" type baits (e.g., Bass Pro's unreleased "Urch X") are LESS effective for these moving/suspended fish — urchin-style baits are a "target bait" best for slow-creeping, non-moving fish, not for fast-moving open-water suspended schools. This directly qualifies/limits other pros' urchin recommendations for this same window.
  - Rod/reel: CarbonLite Technique Series "FFS Minnow" rod, 7 ft 3 in, medium-light action, paired with a Johnny Morris Signature Series 2000-size spinning reel — same rod/reel used across the whole 3"-6" minnow bait range.
  - Retrieve/hookset: uses a "reel set" (do not jerk-set; let the fish load the rod and just reel into it) — states jerking causes missed hookups on the thump-bite of a suspended minnow presentation.
- **Locating method**: forward-facing sonar (live-scope), trolling motor speed "about eight," idle/sweep the main lake looking for singles/doubles/schools cruising in open water — must stay mobile since the fish are mobile ("you need to be on the move").
- **Forage cue**: shad ("relating to nothing except the food"); no water temperature given anywhere in the transcript; no explicit time-of-day pattern given (implies all-day technique since dependent on continuous scoping, not a dawn/dusk window).
- **What fails in this window (explicit)**: (1) assuming suspended fish relate to points/bottom structure — they often do not; (2) fishing shallow docks/wood the way one would "up north" — worked for DeFoe in Wisconsin but got him "scoped" (out-fished by sonar-locating opponents) on deeper, largemouth-poor, grassless lakes (Wedowee, AL) and in a later-round Wisconsin lake without much shallow water; (3) using a single minnow size/weight — he stresses rigging MULTIPLE rods (cites teammate "Dustin" having "seven different minnows rigged up") because one weight/size cannot cover the range of depths and distances suspended fish present at.

## Contradictions / open questions (flagged inline)
- DeFoe explicitly downgrades soft "urchin"/creature "spiky ball" baits for this pattern ("just didn't seem like the bait... this bait less relevant... for fish that are moving"), which CONTRADICTS the Wired2Fish Sept 2026 roundup's endorsement of urchins as a top September bait (cached separately: wired2fish-september-2026-lures-roundup.md). Read together: urchins may work for slower, non-suspended, cover-relating bass (docks/vegetation/rock, per Wired2Fish) while minnow/swimbait-on-jighead outperforms specifically on actively moving, open-water suspended schools (per DeFoe) — the two are likely describing different sub-populations of September bass, not a true contradiction, but flagged as [CONTESTED] on "best bait" without qualifying by fish behavior/location.
