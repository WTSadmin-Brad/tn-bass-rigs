## Extracted findings

- **Test variables:** 30-inch leader vs. 12-inch leader, same rig otherwise. Tested in four scenarios: (1) vertical fishing for suspended bass; (2) vertical fishing with weight on bottom for bass holding near bottom; (3) casted-then-reeled-back at angle; (4) bottom composition variants (rock vs vegetation).
- **Scenario 1 — vertical, suspended fish:** Leader length basically has no impact. Both leaders look essentially the same with rod-still hold, finger-tap on rod, gentle rod lift, and rod pop. Natural arm movement provides plenty of action regardless of leader length.
- **Scenario 2 — vertical, weight on bottom:** "Here is where we will see the first real difference that leader length makes." With the long leader, lowering the rod tip creates a gliding/gentle-sinking action because there is enough slack for the lure to glide. The short leader has neither the distance nor the slack for that glide; the lure stays close to the bottom.
- **Scenario 3 — casted-and-dragged (long cast):** "The extreme angle of the line makes the actual lure placement off the bottom something we may not always think about." The long leader keeps the bait gliding noticeably higher off the bottom; the short leader keeps it tight to the bottom at that angle.
- **Scenario 4 — bottom composition:** Short leader works great on rock-bottom lakes (lure stays in the strike zone tight to clean rock). Long leader is "probably the better option on most days" if the lake bed has junk and vegetation — keeps the bait above the snag zone.
- **Author's bottom line:** Leader length is largely irrelevant for true vertical-suspended presentations, but it materially changes the lure's behavior whenever the weight is on bottom — both for vertical near-bottom fish and for any cast-and-drag presentation.
