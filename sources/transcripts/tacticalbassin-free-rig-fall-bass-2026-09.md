## Extracted findings
- **Core technique — the "free rig"**: a soft-plastic rig where a free-sliding (not pegged) drop-shot-style teardrop weight rides in front of the bait, unattached, giving bait/weight separation. Contrasted explicitly against Texas rig (pegged, bait+weight fall together, fast, connected to bottom), unpegged Texas rig (bullet weight is nose-heavy/hydrodynamic, falls at an angle, less "straight down"), and drop shot (bait suspended but held on a taut line, can't glide/shimmy on a slack fall). The free rig's drop-shot-style weight is bottom-heavy/teardrop and falls straight down, imparting a fast weight-fall paired with a slow, natural, gliding/shimmying bait-fall — described as the trigger mechanism.
- **Why it works right now (fall/September, explicitly stated)**: "pressured fish" and "clear water" respond to the subtler, slower, more natural fall versus jigs/Texas rigs/Ned rigs/drop shots bass have "seen boatloads of." Also: because the bait is not rigidly connected to the weight, a biting bass "doesn't feel the resistance" of the weight the way it would on a pegged rig or taut Carolina rig, reducing early rejections.
- **Habitat/location targets, exact as stated**: isolated rock; bluff ends where the river/creek channel swings close to bluff walls; secondary points with hard cover; stump fields; chunk rock; riprap; rock piles — explicitly framed as "coming back from that summer" main-lake-point jig pattern into shallower rock-adjacent fall structure. As fall progresses further, states fish become more pelagic/roam creek-channel backs and creek arms, suspending over bait — but "right now" (per publish date, early Sept) fish/craws/baitfish still share the same rock-adjacent areas.
- **Creek-arm/suspended-bait note**: as nights cool, baitfish in creek arms get active and move to the "guts" of the channel, often suspending; bass position either on bottom/structure below the suspended bait or up with the bait ball. States he picks up the free rig specifically when bass are relating to bottom/structure below a suspended bait ball (as opposed to fishing straight to the suspended fish, e.g., via forward-facing sonar/swimbait — implicitly a different technique for that case, not covered here).
- **Rod/line, baitcaster setup**: 6'10" rod, medium-medium-heavy power, braid main line to fluorocarbon leader; bobber stop placed ~18 in above a hook (or as noted, sometimes further) to cap how far the weight can slide when he wants the rig fishing effectively "a little bit deeper" — otherwise weight free-slides several feet down the line.
- **Retrieve/technique detail**: key tip = let the rig fall on SLACK line so the weight crashes down fast and separates fully from the bait before the bait glides down; can then hop-hop-drop, shake, or drag; states many bites come on the initial slack-line fall, so watch the line for a jump/twitch rather than feeling a thump.
- **Named baits/rigs by category, exact as stated**:
  - Baitfish-profile (early fall): Berkley Flux Gill 3.5", color "Dark Gill," rigged on a 3/0 EWG hook — wide glide-and-flutter fall, described as scented (PowerBait Max) and effective where bluegill is the main forage. Alternative: "Slingshad" (fluke-style, EWG hook, weedless) for a Highland-reservoir deeper presentation. Z-Man Jerk Shadz (ElaZtech, high-float) run with a bobber stop on a 1/2-3/4 oz free rig weight to get the high-float bait down deep while keeping strong bait/weight separation and fluke action "at depths these fish have never seen it before."
  - Urchin/fuzzy baits: Z-Man Fuzzy Nuggets on a 3/16 oz weight with an Owner Sniper finesse hook as a spinning-rod finesse free rig; TMCO baits (unspecified model, called "Herinumi," described as "like a little mouse," floating/very durable, EWG hook) fished around main lake points, marinas, dock pilings, laydowns.
  - Creature baits: Missile Baits Destroyer and Baby Destroyer (curly-cue twisting appendages, low-kick action, chosen specifically for sparse grass where more-active craws foul); X-Zone Stealth Creature (neutrally buoyant, downsized) fished on "bait finesse" (1/8 oz drop shot weight, small hook) specifically named as productive "here on Chickamauga" on high-pressure days when a full-size Destroyer won't get bit.
  - Craws: Adrenaline Craw Jr (3.5", his "number one" free-rig craw, fished vertically around stumps/dock pilings with a lift-and-drop cadence, not dragged on a main-lake point) and Crush City Bronco Bug (bigger claws/appendages, less prone to fouling than Destroyer-style baits, worked through sparse grass with a 1/2-3/4 oz pegged-ish weight capped ~12-18" up).

Still dealing with hot water, hot weather" — matches the shared-context's caution about a tough transition window; his stated solution is downsizing profile/going finesse rather than switching technique category entirely.
